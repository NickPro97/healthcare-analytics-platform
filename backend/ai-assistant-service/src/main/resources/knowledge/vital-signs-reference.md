# Adult vital signs - reference ranges and derived indices

## Typical resting ranges for adults
Heart rate 60-100 beats/min (NEWS2 scores 0 for 51-90). Respiratory rate 12-20 breaths/min. Systolic blood pressure about 100-139 mmHg and diastolic about 60-89 mmHg. Oxygen saturation 96-100% on room air for most adults; 88-92% may be the target in chronic hypercapnic lung disease. Core temperature about 36.1-38.0 C.

## Mean arterial pressure
MAP is approximately (systolic + 2 x diastolic) / 3. A MAP below 65 mmHg is commonly used as a marker of inadequate organ perfusion and is the vasopressor target threshold in septic shock.

## Shock index
Shock index = heart rate / systolic blood pressure. Normal is roughly 0.5-0.7. Values above about 0.9-1.0 are associated with hypovolaemia, sepsis and higher mortality, and can be abnormal while each vital sign alone still looks acceptable.

## Trends matter
A single value is less informative than its direction. Rising respiratory rate is one of the earliest and most commonly missed signs of deterioration. Falling blood pressure with rising heart rate suggests shock. Always confirm a surprising value (probe position, cuff size, artefact) before acting on it.
