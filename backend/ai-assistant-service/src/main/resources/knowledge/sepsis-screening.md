# Sepsis screening - qSOFA and SIRS

## Definitions (Sepsis-3, 2016)
Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. Organ dysfunction is an acute increase of 2 or more points in the SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) score. Septic shock is sepsis needing vasopressors to keep mean arterial pressure at 65 mmHg or more, with serum lactate above 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation.

## qSOFA (quick SOFA)
One point each for: respiratory rate of 22/min or more; altered mentation (Glasgow Coma Scale below 15); systolic blood pressure of 100 mmHg or less. A score of 2 or more in a patient with suspected infection identifies a higher risk of poor outcome. qSOFA is a prognostic prompt, not a diagnostic test for sepsis; guidance such as the Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 recommends against using qSOFA alone as a single screening tool.

## SIRS criteria
Two or more of: temperature above 38 C or below 36 C; heart rate above 90/min; respiratory rate above 20/min or PaCO2 below 32 mmHg; white cell count above 12,000/mm3, below 4,000/mm3, or more than 10% immature band forms. SIRS is sensitive but not specific: many patients without infection meet it.

## NEWS2 and sepsis
NEWS2 guidance says to consider sepsis in any patient with suspected infection and an aggregate NEWS2 of 5 or more.

## Using platform data
Respiratory rate and systolic BP are available from vital signs, so two of the three qSOFA items can be checked. Mental status is not captured. Lactate and white cell counts are not ingested by this platform; they must be checked in the laboratory system.
